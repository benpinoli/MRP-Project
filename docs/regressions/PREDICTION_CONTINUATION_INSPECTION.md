Independent annual residual aggregation of saved H1 forecasts gives actual-minus-prediction mean at horizon 20 of -1.287 pp (2015), -1.212 (2020), +1.329 (2023), and +1.116 (2025). Annual mean predicted change opposes the annual mean realized change in all four examples. The 20-session outcomes overlap strongly, so these are descriptive regimes, not counts of independent observations. Potential rival explanations include changing level calibration, trend persistence, shifts in volatility, asynchronous prices, and chance. This is not proof that any one predictor failed causally.

A bounded discriminating batch could contrast (i) one-session latest-change-only adjustment with persistence and level AR on identical origins, and (ii) rolling-window level adjustment with expanding A0, with exact windows and candidates frozen before fitting. The former asks whether local reversal survives without a global level anchor; the latter asks whether finite-memory calibration helps. Both require past-only features, training-only scaling, horizon label maturity, matched-origin metrics and uncertainty. Do not present these adaptive tests as fresh independent validation or as evidence of causality or executable returns. Short-term reversal is consistent with multiple microstructure and demand mechanisms.

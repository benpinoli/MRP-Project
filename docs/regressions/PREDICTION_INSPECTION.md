Forecasting evaluation should compare identical origins with persistence and AR, not infer performance from retrospective p-values. Campbell and Thompson's primary study illustrates both limited out-of-sample gains and prolonged underperformance despite genuine predictability: https://www.nber.org/papers/w11468. Shleifer and Vishny's limits-of-arbitrage model motivates a rival mechanism, but a statistical forecast does not identify it or establish trading profits: https://www.nber.org/papers/w5167. Trading feasibility also needs executable prices, class-specific costs, borrow and other constraints, none established here.

The frozen uncertainty procedure bootstraps paired absolute-loss differences on the full proxy grid using 20/60-session blocks (40/60 for horizon 20), including holes; it runs both main and strict masks. This is conditional descriptive uncertainty with fixed forecasts and fixed selected model, not a refitting or selection-adjusted interval. Stationarity and block lengths are approximations, and vintage/calendar caveats persist.
